Delete your account

Updated 14 September 2026

This page covers the three apps published by Washateria LLC — Repple, Repple Coach and Repple Studio. They share one account and one database. Deleting the account deletes it in all three; there is no way to leave one and keep another.

The short version. Ask in the app, or email us from the address on the account. Your account is removed from sign-in and the records that are yours go with it. A short list of gym business records survives with you detached from them — the row stays and the link to you is emptied. Detached is not anonymous: several of those rows keep your name in a column of their own, on purpose, and this page names every one of them. Requests are actioned within 30 days and can be withdrawn until they are. Nothing happens on a timer, though, so email as well as tapping the button.

How to request deletion

In the app

  • Open Repple and sign in.
  • Go to the Me tab.
  • Tap Settings.
  • Under Your data, tap Delete my account.
  • Confirm with Request deletion. You are signed out immediately.

All three apps carry it. In Repple Coach it is Profile › Account › Settings, and in Repple Studio it is Ops › Settings. It is the same account and the same outcome whichever you use.

By email

  • Write to support@repplefitness.com from the email address on the account. A request from any other address is refused, because we have no way to tell it is you.
  • Use the subject Delete my account.
  • Tell us which app you use. It does not change what gets deleted — it helps us find the right account.

You do not have to give a reason, and we will not ask for one.

support@repplefitness.com

What happens after you ask

  • The request is recorded against your account and the account is flagged.
  • It is actioned within 30 days.
  • Until it is actioned you can withdraw it. In the app, sign in and open Me → Settings → Your data, where a pending request is shown with the day you made it and a Withdraw my deletion request button. Or email support@repplefitness.com from the same address and say so. That is the grace period, and it ends when the deletion runs.
  • Asking a second time does not restart the clock, and does not shorten it either. The first request is the one that counts, and its date is the one we work to. Withdrawing and then asking again does start a fresh 30 days, because that is a new request.
  • Once it has run there is nothing to withdraw. Deletion is not reversible and we keep no copy to restore you from.
  • Afterwards the account no longer exists at sign-in. The email address is free again — signing up with it creates a new, empty account, not your old one.

If you are a coach and somebody has accepted one of your documents. A coach can publish a document in Repple Coach — a waiver, a PAR-Q, a studio policy — and clients accept it or are sent it. An acceptance is deliberately locked to the document it is an acceptance of, so that neither can be altered afterwards without the other. That lock still refuses every ordinary route to removing an acceptance — no admin screen, no cleanup, no policy. There is now exactly one thing that can release one, and it is the erasure of the coach who owns the document. So the deletion runs to completion on its own; you do not have to ask us for anything. It used to be the other way round: until that route was built the attempt raised a database error and rolled back, taking even the record that it had been attempted with it. The consequence is stated here rather than discovered afterwards — when your account is erased, the record that your clients accepted your document goes with it. The alternative was keeping your name, your file and your paperwork indefinitely because somebody once ticked a box, and that is not retention.

How that 30 days actually runs. Nothing deletes you on a timer — no scheduled job carries out deletions, and nothing happens unattended. Tapping the button records the request and flags the account; a person then has to carry it out. From inside the apps the database allows exactly one person to do that, the owner of the gym your account belongs to, so an in-app request waits on that one person opening the deletion queue in Repple Studio and working through it. If your account is not attached to a gym there is no such person, and the in-app route cannot finish it at all. So: email us as well as tapping the button. That request reaches us directly, does not depend on anyone else looking, and is the route we can hold to 30 days. If 30 days pass and you are still able to sign in, tell us and we will finish it.

What is deleted

Deleting the account removes it from authentication, and that sign-in row is what everything else hangs from. Counted from the live catalogue on 14 September 2026, erasing it reaches 118 tables in public and 11 more inside Supabase’s own auth schema — 129 in all. Counted instead from your profile row, which is what a gym’s own records point at, 68 tables are destroyed outright and a further 59 keep their rows with the link to you emptied. Which row a figure is counted from matters, because the two answers are not the same. These are the ones that go:

  • Your identity — name, email address, profile photo.
  • Training — workouts, logged sets, cardio and calorie entries, personal records.
  • Body data — InBody scans, body measurements and progress photos, including the image files themselves.
  • Check-ins, habit logs and food logs.
  • Messages with a coach. The thread belongs to the member's account, so deleting a member account removes the thread from the coach's side too.
  • Coaching links and programs — the link to your coach, and any program assigned to you.
  • Class bookings.
  • Not your gym membership, and not the invoices raised against you. Until 14 September 2026 this page listed both here, among what is destroyed. That was the wrong way round: those rows stay with the gym, the link to you is emptied, and the name they were billed to is kept on purpose. They are in What is kept below, together with the payments the gym recorded, for the same reason.
  • Push notification tokens, so notifications stop.
  • Injuries you recorded, and the record of any document you uploaded about one, including the record of your answer when we asked whether it could be sent to be read as text.
  • Connected-service tokens for WHOOP, Oura and Spotify. Those connections end. Fitbit and Garmin cannot be connected yet, so there is no token for either.

Progress photos, specifically. Progress photos are now stored, so they are now deleted — and that took more than deleting a row. The photo record and the image file are two different things in two different places, and removing a database row does not remove a file. So every deletion, whether you remove one photo or the whole account, records the file’s path the moment the row goes, and the file is then deleted against that record. Nothing is marked deleted until the storage service confirms it: a reply that does not say the object has gone leaves the entry outstanding rather than closing it.

And an alarm fires when an entry stops moving. A scheduled run reporting that it ran is not the same as reporting that it removed something, so a second job reads the queue once a day and fails on purpose if anything has been sitting in it for more than a day. A job that fails is a red line in the log of scheduled runs, naming the file and the last thing the storage service said about it. That is the honest size of it: a signal in a place we look, not a message that wakes somebody at three in the morning. What it rules out is the failure this queue had already had once — a drain reporting success every five minutes for three days while one entry, a test row as it turned out, never moved.

The other files are on that mechanism now. Injury documents you uploaded, anything sent as an attachment in a message and your profile photo are recorded and queued the moment your account goes, exactly as a progress photograph is, and a scheduled job sends a delete for each of them within minutes. If you coach through Repple Coach, so are your logo, any document you published and any exercise clip you recorded. The same rule holds for all of them: an entry is closed only when the storage service confirms the object is no longer there. A file that has been asked for is not yet a file that has gone, and we record the second of those, not the first. Both apps say so on their own deletion screens at the moment you ask.

One store is deliberately not on it: your gym’s own filing cabinet. A gym can file a document against a member — a signed contract, an incident report, a photograph — and those are stored under the gym rather than under you. Deleting your account empties the link to you, so nothing in our database joins the document to your account any more — but the file itself stays, and so does whatever a member of staff typed on the row. A title or a note can still carry your name: a gym wrote those, not us, and detaching a row does not go into free text. That is not an oversight and it is not settled either: how long a gym must keep a record about a former member has a different answer in each country, and until that is decided per gym we would rather keep a file a gym may be legally required to hold than destroy it. If you want to know what your gym holds about you, ask them before you delete — afterwards the link that would find it is the thing the deletion emptied.

Nobody sees them unless you share that photo. Progress photos are the one thing in Repple a coach has no standing access to. There is no permission that comes with being your coach; there is a grant you make for one photograph at a time, which you can withdraw, and which ends by itself when the coaching link does. It is enforced at both layers — the record and the file are locked separately, and the rule on the file is written in terms of the rule on the record so the two cannot come apart. Sharing a photo also shares the weight and body-fat figure stored alongside it.

Apple Health is a permission held on your iPhone, not data we store a key for. Deleting the account does not touch it. Revoke it in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Repple, or by removing the app. Anything imported from Health into your training log is deleted with the log.

What is kept

A few records survive, with you detached from them. The row stays; the column that pointed at your account is emptied, and nothing else on the row is changed. That emptied link no longer reaches your email, your profile or anything you logged. But detached is not anonymous. Several of these rows keep your name in a column of their own, written there deliberately, and the next paragraph says which. A gym has to keep financial and attendance records for tax and legal reasons, and cannot do that if removing a member erases them.

Which records keep your name, and why. On the three financial records — an invoice, a payment and a membership — the name it was billed to, taken from or held by is copied onto the row in the moment before your profile goes, because an invoice made out to nobody does not balance a gym’s books and an auditor cannot reconcile a payment from an unnamed party. Three more keep a name that was never a copy of your profile to begin with: the holder’s name written on a guest pass at the desk; the name signed on a gym’s own paperwork — a waiver, a PAR-Q, a policy, a guardian’s consent — because a waiver nobody signed is not evidence of anything; and the names on a staff role a gym granted, so its own record of who let whom in stays readable. A gym’s activity feed is the last of them: each entry’s wording is composed when it is written, so an entry that named you still reads the same afterwards. In every one of these the name is a name on a record and nothing more — it points at no account, opens nothing and is attached to nothing you did in the app. It is on this page because finding it out afterwards would be worse.

What is kept after an account is deleted, and why
What staysWhy
Payments a gym recordedMoney actually received. Gyms must keep it for tax and accounting.
Door-log visitsAttendance and capacity records the gym relies on and may be required to hold.
Guest passes issued or redeemedThey were paid for and have to reconcile against takings. Kept with the holder’s name as it was written at the desk — that name was never a copy of your profile, so there is nothing on the row for the erasure to empty.
Invoices a gym raisedWhat you were billed and whether it was settled. Kept with the name it was billed to, for the reason above.
Memberships you heldWhich plan, from when, and in what state — the line the payments above reconcile against. Kept with the name that was on it.
Paperwork you signed for a gymA waiver, a PAR-Q, a policy, a guardian’s consent. Nothing cascades into these on purpose: a gym has to be able to produce what was signed, and the name that signed it is the signature. Kept with that name, and with a guardian’s where there was one. This is a gym’s own paperwork; a document a coach published and you accepted is a different record, and that one goes when the coach’s account does.
Training packages you boughtA purchase record. Kept for the same accounting reasons as a payment, with your name removed.
Feedback you submittedKept so it still counts, no longer attributed to you.
Crash and error reportsDiagnostics we use to fix faults. The row stays and the pointer to your account is emptied, so what is left is the fault and not the person. While your account exists these are readable by your gym’s owner and not by you, which is why they are not in your export.

Alongside these, a handful of administrative traces survive the same way — who wrote a gym announcement, who sent or accepted an invitation, which coach was named on a nutrition plan or an assigned program. In each of those the row stays and the pointer to your account is emptied. Whether a name is left behind with it is a property of the row rather than a rule: the records named above keep one, and the rest are left holding a pointer that now points at nothing.

We apply no additional retention period on top of these. They are kept for as long as the gym's own accounting and legal obligations require, and are held under the same terms as everything else in Privacy. Security & data shows how far the deletion reaches through the database, and why the files in storage have to be chased separately from the rows.

Take a copy first

The app will export the records held against your account as a JSON file — Me → Settings → Your data → Export my data. It covers your profile, workouts, food logs, measurements, InBody scans, check-ins, habit logs, coach messages, coach nutrition plans, assigned programs, class bookings, referrals and feedback. It now reaches the gym-side records too — membership, invoices, payments, charges, passes and door-log visits. Four things it does not carry: any note a coach wrote about you while you were a lead of theirs; the notifications the app has sent you; the push tokens that identify your handset; and the files themselves. The bundle lists every file held for you, and you save those one at a time from the same screen. Crash reports are not in it either, for the reason given in the table above — while your account exists they are not yours to read. Do it before you request deletion. Afterwards there is nothing left to export, and we cannot produce one for you.

Two things deletion does not do

  • It does not cancel a subscription bought through the App Store or Google Play. Those are cancelled in your Apple or Google account, and will keep billing if you leave them running.
  • It does not settle anything with a gym or a coach. Money owed, notice periods and contracts are between you and them, on their terms.
