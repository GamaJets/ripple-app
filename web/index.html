Why Repple exists

Built by someone the
software kept failing.

Repple was not designed for gyms from the outside. All three apps came from being on the receiving end of software that did not work — as somebody training, as somebody being coached, and as somebody's member.

Training

Meal plans in one app, workouts in another

Measurements in a third. Progress somewhere else again. Every one of them recorded something true, and not one could say what to do next — because none of them could see the others.

Being coached

Waiting on a reply to book a session

And no shared view of progress underneath it, so every conversation began by re-establishing where we were against a goal we had already agreed.

Being a member

A gym app years past its best

The people it fails hardest are the members it was bought for. An owner should be able to hand their clients something that works — and see what it tells them.

Three frustrations, one shape: the information already existed, and nothing read it together. So Repple is one record with three ways in — and it only tells you what to do next because what sits underneath it is real.

Why the split works

One row written once,
read three different ways.

A one-to-one session is a single row. It carries who trained, who coached, when it started and what happened to it. Three apps read that row and none of them asks the same question of it. Nothing is copied between them, so there is nothing to fall out of step.

Repple

Writes most of it

Sets, reps, load, cardio, meals, scans, measurements. The member is the one holding the phone during the work, so they are the one who creates the rows.

Repple Coach

Reads it, and writes the plan

A coach writes programmes and marks what happened in a session. They read a client's log only where that client has linked them.

Repple Studio

Reads the parts that cost money

Attendance, delivered sessions, memberships, payments, door visits. Studio never opens a member's training log — that is not the gym's row to read.

Why it has to be one system

A gym is not a stack of
separate problems.

Attendance is a coaching problem until it becomes a revenue problem. A trainer's schedule is a staffing problem until it becomes a churn problem. When those live in different tools, you find out in the wrong order — usually at the end of the month.

Member logs

The work that happened

Sets, reps, load, effort, cardio, time in heart-rate zone. Imported from the watch or typed in by hand.

Coach sees

Who is drifting

Not a wall of numbers — the three clients whose pattern changed this week, and what changed.

Owner sees

What it costs and earns

Sessions delivered, classes filled, payroll owed, memberships lapsing. The same events, priced.

One Tuesday, followed all the way through

  1. 1

    Monday — the class goes on the board

    Built once as a repeating series rather than twelve copies of the same hour. Its capacity is fourteen, and the equipment register is what says fourteen is still true.

  2. 2

    Tuesday morning — members book

    Eleven places taken. That is a fill rate of 79%, which is a measured number, not the front desk's impression of a busy week.

  3. 3

    18:00 — the desk ticks people off

    Nine of the eleven arrive. Fill is 79% and show is 82%, and they are two different questions with two different fixes. Collapsing them into one word is how a class reads 79% on one screen and 82% on another.

  4. 4

    18:40 — two more people are in the building

    They did not book anything; they came in and trained on the floor. The door log has them, so their attendance is not lost and neither of them looks like a member who has stopped coming.

  5. 5

    Wednesday — a one-to-one is still unmarked

    Tuesday's 17:00 finished and nobody recorded what happened to it. Until somebody does, the gym's payroll figure for the month is a dash with a reason attached, and the session sits at the top of the Sessions screen waiting.

  6. 6

    Month end — nothing has to be reconciled

    Payroll counts sessions marked delivered, priced at the fee the gym set. Revenue counts payments actually recorded. Retention reads the same attendance the desk ticked off. Three answers, one set of rows, nothing exported between them.

The rule we build to

If we don't know the number,
you get a dash — never a guess.

Plenty of software fills a gap with a plausible figure. We think that is worse than useless in a business where you make staffing and pricing decisions off the screen. Every figure in Repple traces to a row someone actually created: a logged session, a recorded payment, a measurement taken. When the input is missing, the app says so and tells you what to enter.

What it replaces

A spreadsheet, a printout
and a group chat.

Most gyms are already running every one of these jobs. The question is not whether they get done — it is whether doing them leaves a row behind that anything else can read. This is the same list of jobs, done the usual way and done here.

The jobSpreadsheet, printout, group chatRepple
Taking a booking A message in a chat, a name written on a printout, and a capacity nobody can check until the room fills up. A place taken against a class with a stated capacity. Fill rate stops being an impression and becomes a number.
Marking who turned up Often not done at all. Attendance is remembered rather than recorded, and only for the classes somebody thought to count. Ticked off at the desk, plus a door log for everyone who walked in without booking. It is the row a drifting member shows up in first.
Paying a coach Sessions counted back out of a diary at the end of the month, with a conversation about the ones nobody wrote down. Counted from sessions marked delivered. Anything still unmarked stops the total and names itself, rather than being rounded into it.
Spotting who is drifting Somebody notices, or nobody does. Usually the cancellation is the first hard evidence. Days since each member was last seen, read from the class register and the door log together.
Knowing what a room holds A number written on a laminated sheet some years ago, which nobody revisits when kit breaks. Checked against the equipment register, so kit out of service changes the capacity the gym measures itself against.
Finding last year's figures A spreadsheet somebody has since renamed, on a laptop that belonged to a manager who left. In the same record as this year, because it was imported at the start rather than typed forward a month at a time.
A member's own training In a different app entirely, or in a notebook. Not the gym's problem and not visible to the coach either. Theirs in Repple, readable by a coach they chose to link, and never the gym's to read set by set.

No claim here is that gyms do this badly. It is that a job done without leaving a row behind cannot be added up later, and every question worth asking about a gym is a question about adding rows up.

Money

Held as whole minor units

Every amount in the system is an integer number of cents or fils, never a decimal. Floats do not belong near a ledger: 0.1 plus 0.2 is a rounding error in a spreadsheet and a dispute in a gym.

Shared functions

One implementation per figure

The web console and the phone app call the same functions over the same rows through the same permissions. A number cannot read one way on the desk and another way on the owner's phone, because there is only one of it.

Straight answers

Do I need all three apps?

No. Install the one that matches what you do. A member never needs Coach; a coach without a gym never needs Studio. They share an account, so if your role changes you sign in to a different app with the same details.

Is Studio an app or a website?

Both, and they are not two products. The phone app is for looking; the web console is for working — wide tables, a keyboard, and the jobs that take twenty rows at a time, like clearing a month of unmarked sessions or pasting in a price list. Both call the identical functions over the identical rows, so no figure can differ between them.

We already run on something else. Can we bring our records across?

Plans and payments import from a CSV, and the import is a dry run until you press the button. It asks which way round your dates are written rather than guessing, and hands back any row it cannot read with confidence, with the reason. Members are checked but not created — a membership has to point at a real account, so members come in by invitation. How it works sets out the whole setup in the order it happens.

What happens if a coach forgets to mark a session?

The month's payroll figure becomes a dash and says how many sessions are outstanding. It does not quietly settle the rest and leave the forgotten ones out. Everything awaiting an outcome sits at the top of the Sessions screen with the buttons that clear it.

Does a member have to book a class to be counted as attending?

No. The door log records entries at the desk, by QR or from the app, whether or not anything was booked. That matters beyond the headline count: without it, a member who switched from classes to training on the floor is indistinguishable from a member who stopped coming.

Does it work without an Apple Watch?

Yes — everything can be entered by hand. What a watch adds is live heart rate during a session and automatic import of workouts you already recorded. One thing it uniquely enables is the heart-rate zone breakdown, which needs samples taken during the session; devices that only report a daily average can't produce it, and Repple won't fake one from a daily figure.

Who can see a member's data?

The member, always. A coach they have deliberately linked to. And their gym, for the membership and attendance information needed to run the place — not private messages, and not their training set by set. Nobody else, and never for advertising. The full detail is in Privacy, and the mechanism that enforces it is in Security & data.

Can a gym export its own data?

Yes. It is your operating record, not ours. Ask and we export everything we hold for your gym.

Are the charts on this page real?

No, and every one of them says so. Repple has not launched publicly, so there is no customer gym to draw from — and we would not publish one if there were. The figures are invented to show the shape of each chart and what it is for. Inside the product, a new account starts empty and fills only with what you record.

What does it cost?

Pricing isn't published yet — the apps are in their first release and we would rather set it against how gyms actually use them than guess in advance. Email support@repplefitness.com and we'll talk plainly about it. Pricing sets out what can honestly be said in the meantime — including that a gym is billed per site rather than per member, and that nothing is charged today.
