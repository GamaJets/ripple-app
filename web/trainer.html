jobs behind each one.

The roster

The clients who need you,
at the top of the list.

A roster sorted alphabetically tells you nothing. Repple Coach flags a client when their adherence drops below 80 per cent, or when nothing at all has been logged for two days — and it says which of the two it is, so you open the conversation knowing what to ask.

  • Adherence, from their own check-in — the weekly rating they gave, turned into a percentage. A client who has never checked in has no adherence at all, and is not quietly counted as perfect.
  • Last active means last anything — a scan, a workout or a check-in. Whichever came most recently is what the row reports, in hours or days.
  • Segments you make yourself — at-risk, online and in-person are built in; add your own tags and each becomes a filter, with a count.
  • Act on a whole segment — message everyone in it, or assign one programme template to all of them, without opening eighteen records.
  • Requests from people looking for a coach — if you have listed yourself, requests arrive as a card with accept and decline on it. Accepting is what adds them to the roster.
One client

The drop is the symptom.
The check-in is the reason.

Open a client and you get what they actually did, not what they told you they did. Their logged sessions, their loads, their scans, their measurements, their meals if they share them — and the notes and feedback you have already given them, on the same timeline.

  • Body composition, segment by segment — fat and lean mass, body water, protein and minerals, and lean mass in each arm, the trunk and each leg, read from their InBody scans.
  • Left–right imbalance, called out — when one side is a tenth behind the other, the screen names it and tells you to cue the weaker side, instead of leaving you to spot it in a column of numbers.
  • Injuries they disclosed — held with the client, and the plan flags and swaps movements that load those areas until they mark it recovered. Guidance, not medical advice.
  • Two kinds of note — feedback the client reads, and private notes only you can see. Both land on the same timeline, newest first, so the history of a relationship is one list.
  • Their last six meals — with the calories and how each was logged, so a nutrition conversation starts from what was eaten rather than what was remembered.
Programmes

Write it once.
Assign it many times.

Rebuilding the same block in a new document for every client is the largest unpaid job in coaching. The builder, the template library, the video library and the nutrition panel exist to end it.

Build

Days, exercises, sets and reps

Add a training day, pick movements from the built-in list or type your own, set the sessions and the rep range. It is a structure you edit, not a document you retype.

Assign

Straight into their Train tab

Assigning pushes the programme into the app the client already opens. No export, no PDF, and no version of it that only you can see.

Reuse

Save it as a template

Any programme you like becomes a template, and a template can go to a whole segment at once. The block you have refined over two years stops living in your head.

Revert

Back to the auto plan

Every client has an automatically built plan behind yours. One tap puts them back on it, so trying something is not a decision you cannot undo.

Demonstrate

Your clip, on the exercise

Record on the phone, upload from the library, or paste a hosted link. Each clip carries an exercise name and plays against that movement for every client who has it.

Adjust

Nutrition without a second tool

Shift a client's daily calories, protein, carbs and fat, assign specific meals to specific slots, and leave a note. It applies to their Meals tab, where they already log.

A clip you remove from the library is removed for every client who could see it, and the app says so before it does it. A clip that has no playable file yet is listed as not recorded rather than shown as live.

The week

A schedule that knows
who is in front of you.

One-to-ones and group classes on a single timetable, with the month in view and the day you tapped underneath it. Tap a booking and you are already in that client's record.

  • Book them, or let them book you — add a session against a client, or open a slot they can take. Weekly availability generates those open slots four weeks ahead in one go.
  • You cannot double-book yourself — a session that overlaps one you already have is refused, and the app names the session it clashes with.
  • A cancellation re-offers the slot — cancel and every client can be told that time is free. Inside 24 hours the app reminds you your own late-cancel policy applies, and is clear that Repple does not charge it for you.
  • Group classes across branches — title, type, room, capacity and a weekly repeat, then a check-in screen you tap as people arrive. Only a tapped name counts as present.
  • Send the week to your own calendar — booked sessions export as a standard calendar file, so the phone you already check knows where you are meant to be.

What happened

A session that finished
is not a session that ran.

The easy way to count sessions is to count the bookings whose start time has passed. It is also how a gym ends up paying for no-shows, slots nobody cancelled and sessions the trainer never turned up to. Repple asks you to say what happened instead — four answers, one tap each — and treats an unmarked session as unknown rather than delivered.

  1. 1

    The queue finds them for you

    A session counts as waiting when it was booked, its end time has passed, and nobody has said what happened. The list reaches ninety days back — far enough to catch a fortnight you forgot about.

  2. 2

    Four answers, not two

    Went ahead, did not turn up, cancelled late, cancelled in time. The last two are separated on purpose: one is usually paid and the other usually is not, and collapsing them is how that argument starts.

  3. 3

    A whole day at once, and undo on each

    Mark a day of sessions in one action when they all went the same way, and undo any of them afterwards. Undo returns the session to waiting — it does not mark it cancelled.

  4. 4

    The rate is stamped as you mark it

    Whatever the session was worth at that moment is saved with it, so a rate change next quarter cannot rewrite what last month cost.

Straight about limits

The things it says plainly
rather than quietly.

Most of these are the sentences a product would normally hide. They are on this page for the same reason they are in the app: finding them out later is worse.

Invites

Repple does not send the email

Inviting a client records the invitation against their address. They are linked to you the first time they sign in to Repple with it — so you still have to tell them. The app says exactly that rather than reporting an email it never sent.

Hand-added clients

They have no Train tab yet

A client you typed in is on your roster and in your schedule, but a programme assigned to them is saved on your device only and a message cannot reach them until they join. Bulk actions report how many actually landed.

Messages

Added to a thread, not confirmed read

A broadcast reports how many client threads it was written to. Whether a push notice arrives depends on their phone settings, so nothing here claims delivery it cannot see.

Money

Your rate is your own arithmetic

The revenue figure on Analytics, and the pay estimate on class check-in, are your rate multiplied by what happened. Repple does not process those fees and says so on both screens.

Trends

Two months before a line is drawn

One real snapshot is recorded per month, and months with no snapshot stay empty rather than being back-filled with today's number. A fresh install has no trend, and says so.

Targets

Unset, not pre-filled

Your revenue and client goals start empty, and your session rate starts at nothing. No screen opens with a suggested figure that belongs to nobody.

One account, whatever you are

Coach at a gym on Tuesday,
train on your own on Thursday.

The same login opens Repple Coach for your client book and Repple for your own training. Your workouts are yours — a gym you coach at sees the sessions you deliver, not the sessions you do, and never the messages between you and a client. If you leave, your clients' records go with the clients, because they always belonged to them.

See the gym's side See the member's side

Listed only if you say so

The Find a Trainer switch is off until you turn it on. Turned on, members browsing for a coach see your name, tagline, bio, specialties and rate, and can send you a real request.

Private stays private

Access is enforced by row-level rules in the database, not by a setting on a screen. Another coach's roster is unreachable from your account, whatever the app asks for.

Export or erase

Take everything Repple holds about you as a file, or ask for the account to be erased. Your clients are not deleted with you — they stay with the gym, and lose you as their coach.

Empty means empty

Where a figure has nothing behind it the app shows a dash and names what is missing — an unset rate, an unmarked session, a client who has never checked in. Loading and empty never look the same.

Straight answers

The questions worth asking before you move a client book into something new.

Do I need to work at a gym?

No. Bring your own clients and Repple Coach is simply your book — roster, programmes, schedule, videos and analytics all work without a gym anywhere near them. If you do coach inside a gym running Repple Studio, the outcomes you mark are what its payroll pays on, and the rest of your book stays yours.

How do clients actually get connected to me?

Three ways. You add them by hand; you record an invitation against their email and they link to you the first time they sign in to Repple with that address; or, if you have listed yourself in Find a Trainer, they send you a request you accept. Repple does not send the invitation email for you — the app says so, so you know to tell them yourself.

What if a client never installs the app?

They still sit on your roster and in your schedule, and you can keep notes and a programme against them. What does not work until they join is the part that needs their phone: a programme you assign is held on your device rather than in their Train tab, and messages cannot reach them. The app tells you that at the moment it happens rather than reporting a success it did not have.

What can I see of a client's data?

What they have shared by connecting to you: their training log, their check-ins, their scans and measurements, their meals if they share them. They can end the link whenever they like, and their record goes with them when they do — it was always theirs.

Where do the numbers come from?

Out of the log, the check-ins and the outcomes you record. Adherence is the rating the client filed. Last active is the most recent thing they logged. Sessions are the ones in your own schedule. Nothing is seeded with sample data, which is why a new account looks empty rather than impressive.

Can I take payment from clients?

You can sell a membership or a pack of sessions and be paid through Stripe, which handles the card details — Repple never sees them. Setting up payouts is a Stripe onboarding step inside the app, and until it is finished the screen says payouts are not live rather than showing you a balance.

What does it cost?

Pricing isn't published yet — the apps are in their first release and we would rather set it against how people actually use them than guess in advance. Creating an account costs nothing today. Email support@repplefitness.com and we'll talk plainly about it.
